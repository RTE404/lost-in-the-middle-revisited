# Fix the gap statistic before trusting map-reduce

The plan can run on a free Kaggle T4, but as written it cannot show what it sets out to show. Its two success tests are statistically broken. The headline "gap" (best position minus worst position) is **a max−min statistic that is well above zero even when position has no effect**: about **3–4 points at n=500**, about **8 points for 100 KV items**. Its bootstrap CI "excludes 0" in **32–100% of null runs**, depending on how it is computed. The "average accuracy not worse" rule (CI not entirely below 0) is a backwards non-inferiority test: noisier experiments pass it more easily. The map-reduce reduce step also rests on an assumption the authors' data contradicts. The plan assumes exactly one group holds the answer. In fact **104 to 682 of the 2,655 questions** (3.9–25.7%, depending on the match rule) have a distractor whose text contains a gold answer string. Liu's distractors are same-topic hard negatives, so the 4 gold-free groups will produce verified but wrong answers. With at most one vote per group, **1-vs-1 ties become the typical case**, and a weakly calibrated, fp16-noisy logprob comparison across different prompts ends up deciding the answer. Contiguous grouping also ties the gold's within-group slot (1/1/2/3/4) and the difficulty of the distractors it shares a group with to its global position. A "flattened" curve could therefore be an artefact of the grouping. On top of these problems, the baseline may have nothing to fix. Recent 7–8B models show only **5–7 point** first-vs-last gaps. At n=500 the smallest detectable effect is about **4–7 points**. Literal UUID KV retrieval is probably near ceiling for Qwen at this length. And the KV prompts are really **about 20.4K Qwen tokens**, not 15K. All of this can be fixed: pre-registered linear position contrasts with a difference-in-contrasts test, a margin-based non-inferiority test, slot-balanced grouping, explicit abstention scoring, a comparable-confidence or judge reduce, the full 2,655 questions, and three control arms that separate "position fix" from "fewer distractors" and "new prompt".

## Critical: four defects that decide the verdict before the GPU runs

### C1. The best-minus-worst "gap" is positive even when there is no position effect

**The problem.** The gap takes the best and worst positions from the same noisy accuracies it then tests, so it is at least 0 by construction and biased upward. A simulation of the design with **no true position effect** (5 positions, p≈0.6, n=500) gives a mean "gap" of **2.8 points at 11% pairwise discordance and 3.6 points at 24%**. For the KV design (7 positions, n=100) it gives **7.6 points**, with a 95th percentile of 12.0. When best and worst are re-chosen inside every bootstrap resample, the percentile-bootstrap CI **covered 0 in 0% of replications** (the simulation in `recent_evidence_and_stats.md`). The general statistics literature explains why. The bootstrap is inconsistent for non-differentiable functionals and parameters on a boundary ([Andrews 2000](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-0262.00114/abstract)). The maximum of several means has an irregular limit when positions are near-tied ([Valuing Winners, arXiv 2605.18887](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18887)). And an arm chosen for being extreme suffers a winner's curse that needs conditional or projection inference ([Andrews, Kitagawa & McCloskey, NBER w25456](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25456/w25456.pdf)). The success rule "the gap shrinks, and the paired-bootstrap CI excludes 0" therefore compares two biased statistics whose biases differ. The bias depends on accuracy level and discordance, and those differ between the baseline and map-reduce. A truly flat map-reduce curve would still show a gap of about 3 points.

**Fix.** Pre-register linear contrasts that are fixed in advance and computed per question: U = mean(acc₁, acc₂₀) − mean(acc₅, acc₁₀, acc₁₅); primacy = acc₁ − acc₁₀; recency = acc₂₀ − acc₁₀. The primary success test is the **difference-in-contrasts** ΔU = U_baseline − U_MR. Its CI comes from 10,000 resamples of **question IDs**, with each resampled ID carrying all positions and both arms. An equivalent model-based check is the condition×position interaction in `correct ~ condition × position + (1|question)`. If a spread number is still wanted, report the range minus its **within-question permutation-null mean**, with position labels shuffled within each question. Use it as a descriptive statistic, not as the test ([recent evidence notes, citing Dror et al.](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/)). The existing `litm/stats.py` already has fixed first/middle/last contrasts and no gap function. The plan's own `lost-in-the-middle-plan.md` calls the gap "descriptive only". Keep it that way.

### C2. "CI not entirely below 0" rewards noise instead of proving non-inferiority

**The problem.** The rule passes whenever the CI's upper bound is above 0. A method 8 points worse with a wide CI passes, and a smaller or noisier run passes more easily. Non-inferiority means the **lower** confidence bound of (new − reference) stays above a **pre-specified, justified margin −δ** ([FDA NI guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/78504/download); [EMA NI margin guideline](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-choice-non-inferiority-margin_en.pdf)). This criterion carries more weight than it looks. The only head-to-head comparison of aggregators found shows **independent-worker voting ("Merge") 8–11 points below** sequential and hierarchical aggregation: HotpotQA 42.96 vs 50.62/53.62, MuSiQue 26.66 vs 29.40/37.09. The authors' explanation is that "each worker can only maintain the information in its own chunk" ([Chain-of-Agents, arXiv 2406.02818](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818)). An average-accuracy loss is a live possibility.

**Fix.** Pre-register δ_NI, for example 2 points absolute, justified as smaller than the middle-position gain you expect. Require the lower bound of the two-sided 95% paired CI of mean-over-positions(acc_MR − acc_base) to be above −δ_NI. Average the 5 positions per question first, then bootstrap questions; never treat the 2,500 question×position items as independent. Note that a 5-position average gives the two end positions 2/5 of the weight.

### C3. The baseline may have no measurable gap to fix

**The problem.** The design assumes there is a gap for map-reduce to shrink. Recent evidence says any gap will be small. Text-only NQ-Open multi-document QA on 2025–2026 7–8B readers shows first/middle/last of **55.0/50.4/47.6** (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), **55.0/51.2/49.8** (InternVL3-8B) and **62.0/59.2/55.6** (Qwen3-VL-8B). That is a primacy-leaning 5–7 point effect, with middle ≈ last, not the 20+ point U of 2023 models ([Lost at the End, arXiv 2606.16494v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16494v3)). Compare Vicuna-7B-16k's 71.9 → 47.3 → 50.7 ([Hsieh et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16008)). The minimum detectable paired difference at 80% power is (z₁₋α/₂ + z₀.₈)·√(d/n), where d is discordance ([Card et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.745/)). At n=500 it is **4.0/5.6/6.9 points for d = 0.10/0.20/0.30**, and **8.9–15.3 points for KV at n=100**. ΔU is a difference of two contrasts, so its variance is higher and its MDE larger still. The KV arm is probably at ceiling. Qwen2.5 is "nearly perfect on retrieval-based long-context tasks" ([Yu et al., arXiv 2406.02536](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02536)). Qwen3-4B non-thinking scores **91.0 on RULER at 16K**, and RULER averages harder tasks than single-key UUID lookup ([Qwen3 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388)). Chroma found "no notable variation" across 11 needle positions for literal NIAH ([Context Rot](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)). With minimal lexical overlap, performance falls from about 98% to 44.1 at 4K for Llama-3.1-8B, and a lost-in-the-middle pattern appears across depth ([NoLiMa, arXiv 2502.05167](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05167)). Relative length matters too. The effect is strongest at 25–50% of the context window ([Veseli et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07479)), and 20 documents fill only about 9% of Qwen2.5-3B's 32K window.

**Fix.** Use **all 2,655 questions**. This roughly halves the SE, giving an MDE of about 1.7–3.0 points. The earlier budget analysis put the extra cost at about 3–4 GPU-hours per model. Add a **30-document arm**, which is part of Liu's original protocol, as the "is there anything to fix" test ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03172)). Optionally run 60–120 documents (about 8–16K tokens) for Qwen2.5-3B. For KV, pilot 20–50 items at the first and middle positions before committing. If accuracy is 95% or higher, demote literal UUID KV to a sanity check and add a NoLiMa-style non-literal arm. Use the authors' 500 KV items with a seeded sample, not 100. Pre-register "baseline shows no significant U/primacy/recency contrast" as an outcome that makes the map-reduce comparison moot rather than a "success".

### C4. Gold-free groups will outvote or tie the gold group, so the tie-breaker becomes the decision rule

**The problem.** The plan assumes one group holds the answer and the others say NOT FOUND. Neither holds. The authors' `hasanswer` flag is False on every distractor, but string matching over the local copies of the authors' 20-document files finds answer-bearing distractors in **104 questions (text-only whole-word match), 503 (title+text) and 682 (the metric's own character-substring rule)**. That is 126 of a seed-0 500-question sample. They are concentrated in the highest-ranked distractors: in the gold-at-10 file, **584 / 465 / 291 / 382 / 357** such questions for groups 0–4. (These counts come from a local analysis of the files, not a published source; see [the generator script](https://github.com/nelson-liu/lost-in-the-middle/blob/main/scripts/make_qa_data_from_retrieval_results.py), which picks distractors by DPR's `hasanswer`.) Beyond real answer strings, same-topic hard negatives invite confident wrong answers. In a 2026 study of 3.8–8B models with an abstention prompt like the plan's, models answered only **0–3%** of the time with no gold passage present. With one fluent misleading passage, they answered **13.6–74.3%** of the time (about 41.6% on average), and **63%** of those answers echoed the planted wrong entity. The authors conclude that prompt abstention checks "whether context is sufficient, not whether it is correct" ([arXiv 2608.22228](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22228)). Evidence verification cannot catch this, because a distractor can genuinely contain the wrong entity. Verification removes fabricated quotes, not wrong answers that are grounded in the text. With one vote per group, the usual result is one correct candidate against zero to four distinct wrong ones. **Ties at count 1 are therefore the main case**, and two distractor groups agreeing on the same wrong year can outvote the gold 2–1.

The tie-breaker is not strong enough for that job. It compares mean answer-token logprobs produced under **different prompts**. Instruction-tuned models push token confidences close to 1, and QA sequence likelihoods are miscalibrated ([Calibrating Sequence Likelihood, arXiv 2210.00045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00045)). LLM×MapReduce lost about 2–5 points on several tasks when it dropped in-context confidence calibration, and up to 35 points on one task when it also dropped its structured protocol ([arXiv 2410.09342](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09342)). Greedy fp16 logprobs also shift with batch composition. Near-tied logits cause up to 9% accuracy variation across batch and GPU configurations ([Yuan et al., arXiv 2506.09501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09501)), so near-ties between groups can flip between reruns. The fallback, "most confident unverified answer", puts back exactly the hallucinations that verification was meant to remove.

One suggested fix introduces a new bug. The repo audit proposes breaking ties by **earliest group**. That rule favours the gold at position 1 and so builds primacy into the reduce step: it would create the very position effect the study is trying to measure. Do not use it, or any other order-dependent tie-break.

**Fix.** First, measure the map stage directly: report the NOT FOUND rate on gold-free groups and the false-abstention rate on gold groups, per model. Second, make confidence comparable across candidates. Rescore every verified candidate under **one common prompt**: P(answer | question, its evidence sentence), or P("Yes") for "Does this sentence answer the question?". Or run a short **LLM judge** over at most 5 shuffled candidates with their evidence. That prompt is about 200 tokens, too short for a position effect of its own. All three reduce variants reuse the same map outputs, so comparing them costs nothing extra ([CoA](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818); [LangChain map_rerank](https://python.langchain.com/api_reference/langchain/chains/langchain.chains.combine_documents.map_rerank.MapRerankDocumentsChain.html)). **Pre-register one variant as primary** to avoid forking paths, and label the others as sensitivity analyses. Third, report the AUROC of mean logprob for correct-vs-wrong among verified candidates. If it is near 0.5, the vote-plus-logprob rule is no better than chance and must not be primary. Fourth, add an **oracle-reduce upper bound** (correct if any group's candidate is correct), which separates map failures from reduce failures. Fifth, report accuracy separately for items decided by the vote, the tie-break and the fallback, and for the subset of questions with no answer-bearing distractor. If the fallback stays, report results with and without it.

### C5. Contiguous grouping ties within-group slot and distractor difficulty to global position

**The problem.** The authors' distractors appear in the **same order in all five position files** and are sorted by retrieval rank. That held for 2,655 of 2,655 questions in the local analysis. Each gold position therefore maps to a fixed group, slot and set of group-mates:

| Global gold position | Group | Within-group slot | Distractors sharing its group |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | d1–d3 (hardest) |
| 5 | 2 | 1 | d5–d7 |
| 10 | 3 | 2 | d9–d11 |
| 15 | 4 | 3 | d13–d15 |
| 20 | 5 | 4 | d17–d19 (easiest) |

A leftover 4-document primacy or recency effect, or easier group-mates at later positions, would make the map-reduce curve look like a recency slope, or hide one. This confounds exactly the quantity being tested ([recent evidence notes](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07479); local analysis of the [authors' data](https://github.com/nelson-liu/lost-in-the-middle)).

**Fix.** Keep the groups contiguous, which preserves the "original order" at group level. Then permute documents **within each group** with a fixed seed so that the gold occupies each of the 4 slots equally often at every global position. Log `gold_group`, `gold_slot` and `n_answer_bearing_in_group` for every item, and report accuracy per slot as a diagnostic. If order-preserving groups are required, add a control with strided groups (i, i+5, i+10, i+15) or shifted boundaries. Identify the gold with `ctx["isgold"]`, never with `hasanswer`.

### C6. Scoring the wrong string silently inflates accuracy

**The problem.** `best_subspan_em` counts a prediction as correct when any normalized gold answer is a **character substring** of the normalized prediction ([metrics.py](https://github.com/nelson-liu/lost-in-the-middle/blob/main/src/lost_in_the_middle/metrics.py)). The authors keep only the first line, specifically to stop models from gaming the metric by copying context ([evaluate_qa_responses.py](https://github.com/nelson-liu/lost-in-the-middle/blob/main/scripts/evaluate_qa_responses.py)). The local analysis found degenerate answers that break naive scoring:

| Item (gold_at_0 line) | Gold answers | Scored correct |
|---|---|---|
| idx 1451 | includes `"*"`, which normalizes to `""` | every prediction, including "NOT FOUND" |
| idx 1840 | `"S"` | "Answer: NOT FOUND" |
| idx 967 | `"one"` | "none" |

Beyond these, **63 questions have a shortest normalized answer of 2 characters or fewer** and 368 are purely numeric. If an Evidence line, a raw map transcript or a concatenation of group answers ever reaches the scorer, short answers like "14" or "0" will match by accident. The metric also biases average accuracy **against** map-reduce: verbose baseline answers pick up substring credit that short reduced answers do not ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03172)).

**Fix.** Score only the final reduced answer string, with the "Answer:" prefix removed. Represent abstention as `final_answer=""` with `abstained=True`, and score it 0 **before** calling the metric. Add unit tests for idx 1451 and 1840. Treat idx 1451 the same way in both arms: either keep it and document it (it is a constant, so it cancels in paired contrasts) or exclude it from both. Report the mean output length per arm, plus a strict metric (normalized EM or token F1) as a secondary. Rescore the baseline truncated to its first sentence to measure how much verbosity inflates it. For KV, never score a concatenation of group outputs: the UUID would match whenever the gold group answered at all, whatever the vote decided.

## Important: attribution, verification and inference-stack faults

### I1. The map-reduce arm changes three things at once

The arm changes the prompt (NOT FOUND plus Answer/Evidence lines), the number of distractors per call (3 instead of 19) and the context length (about 670 instead of about 3,090 mean Qwen tokens, measured locally). Without controls, a better middle-position score cannot be credited to "fixing position". Map-reduce may even **beat baseline position 1** through distractor reduction alone. The closest prior number is modest: Chain-of-Agents narrowed the NQ position spread only from **6.13 (±2.17) to 4.89 (±1.91)**, and the ± ranges overlap ([CoA](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818)). **Fix:** add three controls and reference points. First, **one group of 20 documents with the map prompt**, which isolates the prompt effect. Second, a **4-document baseline** with the gold at a fixed slot, which isolates the effect of fewer distractors. Third, closed-book and oracle runs as the floor and ceiling. Report the share of the middle-position deficit recovered, (MR_mid − base_mid)/(base_pos1 − base_mid), with a percentile-bootstrap CI, and flag it as unstable if the denominator's CI touches 0. Frame the contribution as a careful small-model replication and ablation. The method itself is not new: LangChain map_rerank, LLM×MapReduce, Chain-of-Agents and per-document sub-agent RAG all precede it ([LLM×MapReduce](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09342); [SPD-RAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08329)).

### I2. Evidence verification has unspecified edge cases in both directions

The verification rule is too loose in two places. First, "the answer is inside the evidence" must test `normalize(answer) in normalize(evidence)` **and** require a non-empty normalized answer, because `"" in anything` is True. Second, "the evidence is a substring of the documents" passes trivially for evidence like "1938", so require a minimum length, for example 5 or more normalized tokens. The rule is too strict elsewhere:

- **Sentence splits.** 36 of 2,655 gold passages have no single regex-split sentence that contains the answer.
- **Newlines.** 25 documents contain literal newlines, so evidence can span lines.
- **Titles.** About 400 questions have the answer only in a distractor *title*. Whether titles count as group text is a choice to pre-register.
- **Small edits.** Small models often change quoted text slightly.

ResearchQA uses the same normalize-then-substring design and rejects fuzzy or embedding matching because it "would incorrectly accept fabricated-but-topical quotes". Even frontier models reach only **0.70–0.85** citation accuracy there ([ResearchQA, arXiv 2607.11074](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11074v1)). **Fix:** keep the strict match as the primary check. Add one pre-registered graded fallback: rapidfuzz `partial_ratio` ≥ 90 against a single document span, **and** the answer appears verbatim in the matched *document* span, not only in the model's quote. Tune the threshold on the pilot. Match against title plus text. Parse Evidence from after `Evidence:` to the end of the output. Report the share of correct gold-group answers that verification rejects.

### I3. Two verdict rules decide on selected or non-significant results

The **"worst middle" comparator** picks the minimum of positions 5/10/15 after seeing the data: three implicit tests, biased toward declaring U or primacy. **Fix:** pre-register the middle as position 10, or as the mean of 5/10/15. If the claim is "beats *every* middle position", use an intersection-union test, where all three paired CIs must exclude 0. Apply Holm over contrasts × models × tasks, or name one primary contrast per model. The **"flat" verdict**, defined as both CIs including 0, turns non-significance into a finding. At an MDE of 4–7 points it becomes the default outcome. **Fix:** declare "flat" only when both 90% paired CIs fall inside a pre-registered ±Δ_eq (for example ±3 points, a TOST equivalence test). Otherwise report "inconclusive" ([FDA NI/equivalence logic](https://www.fda.gov/media/78504/download); [earlier power analysis method](https://github.com/dallascard/NLP-power-analysis)).

### I4. Qwen3-4B "thinking off" hides a silent sampling trap

With `enable_thinking=False`, the template writes an empty `<think>\n\n</think>\n\n` block **into the prompt**. Offline `LLM.chat(..., chat_template_kwargs={"enable_thinking": False})` has been supported since vLLM 0.9.0 and applies no reasoning parser ([Qwen vLLM docs](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/latest/deployment/vllm.html); [vLLM LLM API](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/api/vllm/entrypoints/llm.html)). The trap is Qwen3-4B's `generation_config.json`: **do_sample true, T=0.6, top_p 0.95, top_k 20** ([generation_config.json](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B/raw/main/generation_config.json)). vLLM loads it by default ([engine args](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/configuration/engine_args.html)). If `sampling_params` is ever left as None, the "greedy" run quietly samples. There is also an open server-path bug (still open as of April 2026): with `--reasoning-parser qwen3`, the whole answer lands in `delta.reasoning` and `content` is empty ([vllm#40816](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/40816)). Reasoning-tuned models also abstain worse on unanswerable questions ([AbstentionBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09038v1)); that finding has not been shown to transfer to thinking-off mode, but it is a risk. **Fix:** always pass an explicit `SamplingParams(temperature=0.0, max_tokens=150, logprobs=1)`. Use the offline API only. Strip any `<think>…</think>` before parsing. Do not add "/no_think". In the pilot, check that two identical runs are byte-identical and that outputs start with "Answer". The cleaner option, recommended in the earlier review, is **Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507**. It has no think block and the same memory profile, but it is a different model and needs an explicit `max_model_len` because its native context is 262K ([2507 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507)). Whichever model you choose, use it for both the baseline and map-reduce.

### I5. fp16 NaNs appear silently under greedy decoding and would poison the tie-breaker

No report was found of text-only Qwen2.5-3B or Qwen3-4B producing NaNs in fp16 under vLLM, but sibling Qwen models have. On a T4, Qwen2.5-VL-3B crashed intermittently, which the authors attribute to fp16 overflow ([arXiv 2603.13893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13893)). Other Qwen models collapse into repeated "!" tokens (token id 0) and contaminate later requests ([vllm#55291](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/55291); [vllm-ascend#3934](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm-ascend/issues/3934)). Greedy decoding skips the multinomial "inf/nan" crash, so a NaN shows up as a `^!+$` output or a `nan` logprob, and **one NaN turns a mean logprob into NaN**. **Fix:** check every output for NaN or -inf logprobs, `^!+$`, and a missing "Answer:". Re-run flagged prompts. Qwen2.5-3B in fp32 fits a T4 at short `max_model_len`; Qwen3-4B in fp32 does not, so drop and count its flagged items. Restart the engine if the flag rate spikes. Pilot about 200 of the long KV prompts first.

### I6. The KV prompts are about 20.4K Qwen tokens, and two configurations reject them

Over all 500 KV300 prompts, the Qwen2.5-3B tokenizer with its chat template measured **20,239–20,487 tokens** (local analysis). The plan's "about 15K" comes from the GPT tokenizer ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03172)). Two configurations fail on this. The inference-stack notes recommend `max_model_len ≈ 16384`, which would reject **every** KV prompt. The local `run.py` defaults to `--max-model-len 20480`, which rejects **239 of 500** before any GPU work. The KV prompt therefore fills about **62%** of Qwen2.5-3B's 32K window, not about 47%. That is past the point where Veseli et al. see primacy collapse ([Veseli et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07479)). Adjust the expected curve shape and the time budget, which rises by about 35% for KV prefill. **Fix:** measure with each pinned tokenizer and set `max_model_len` to at least about 21,504 for baseline KV. For Qwen3-4B that is about 2.9 GB of KV cache at 144 KiB/token, which still fits one sequence at a time. Set about 2,048 for map calls.

### I7. The inference stack must be pinned now

vLLM PR #55387, still open on 24 September 2026, moves default wheels to CUDA 13.2 and torch 2.13 ([vllm#55387](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/55387)). In September 2026 another project's CUDA-13 wheels silently dropped sm_75 and failed on T4 ([xllamacpp#190](https://github.com/xorbitsai/xllamacpp/issues/190)). **Fix:** pin `vllm==0.18.1` with its torch 2.10/cu128 as validated on Kaggle 2×T4 ([kaggle-vllm](https://github.com/kaggle-vllm/kaggle-vllm)). Force TRITON_ATTN and fp16, and assert at startup that `torch.cuda.get_device_capability()==(7,5)`. Check for a cp313 wheel in case the Kaggle image has moved to Python 3.13; otherwise use a 3.12 venv. A stale note says vLLM's minimum is compute capability 7.0; the current docs say 7.5 ([vLLM install](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/getting_started/installation/gpu.html)).

### I8. The local `litm` code cannot run map-reduce yet

The local suite passes (**30 tests in 27.5 s**), but the code implements the older plan: all 2,655 questions and 5 KV positions. It has the following gaps:

- **Resume key.** It is `(task, position, idx)`, so the five map calls for one item collide on resume, and `analyze.load_scores` overwrites `scores[...][idx]` with whichever group row came last.
- **Sampling.** `--qa-limit` and `--kv-limit` take the first N items, not a seeded sample.
- **KV positions.** `KV_POSITIONS` holds 5 positions, not the design's 7.
- **Logprobs.** `SamplingParams` requests none, and records save only text, token count and finish reason.
- **Prompt builders.** The authors' `get_qa_prompt` hard-codes its template file. `get_kv_retrieval_prompt` **raises ValueError for any group that lacks the query key**, which is 14 of the 15 KV groups ([prompting.py](https://github.com/nelson-liu/lost-in-the-middle/blob/main/src/lost_in_the_middle/prompting.py)).

**Fix:**

- Key the records on `(method, task, position, idx, group)`, and write map outputs to a separate file.
- Make the analysis read only reduced records.
- Add a `Generator.generate(prompts)` layer that returns text, token_ids, logprobs and finish_reason.
- Save a `sample_500.json` of seeded line indices and reuse it for every arm.
- Add a `qa_map.prompt` that puts NOT FOUND and the format lines **before** the final `Answer:` cue, reusing the authors' document-formatter line byte for byte. Read it in text mode, because the `.prompt` files have CRLF line endings on this checkout.
- Add a local KV formatter without the key guard.

### I9. Logprob mechanics need care to be usable at all

vLLM V1 returns **raw** logprobs by default, before any temperature or penalties are applied. Requesting `prompt_logprobs` bypasses the prefix cache and recomputes the full prefill ([V1 guide](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/usage/v1_guide.html)). **Fix:** request `logprobs=1` and never request `prompt_logprobs`. Look up each token's logprob by its sampled token id. Map tokens to the text between `Answer:` and the first newline using cumulative decode offsets, with one explicit rule for tokens that straddle a boundary, and exclude the Evidence tokens. Fix `max_num_seqs` and the input order, and record which rule decided each item. Treat the logprob tie-break as a sensitivity analysis unless its AUROC (C4) justifies it.

### I10. The KV map-reduce needs slot-balanced positions and three-step verification

KV is the clean case. The query key occurs in exactly one record and values are unique, so "evidence key = query key" guarantees any verified answer comes from the gold group. Evenly spaced 7-position sets land the gold on uneven slots, though. For example, [0, 49, 99, 149, 199, 249, 299] maps to slots 0, 9, 19, 9, 19, 9, 19. A 20-pair map prompt is at most about 1.46K tokens, a short lookup that should be near ceiling (local analysis). **Fix:** choose positions with a constant slot, such as **0, 40, 100, 140, 200, 240, 280** (all slot 0), or report the slot for each position. Ask for `Value: <uuid>` plus `Evidence: "<key>": "<value>"`. Verify with a UUID regex, check that the key matches the query and the value matches the answer, and check that the pair is in that group.

### I11. Qwen2.5's template adds a system prompt that Qwen3's does not

When no system message is given, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct's template injects "You are Qwen, created by Alibaba Cloud. You are a helpful assistant." Qwen3-4B's template injects nothing ([Qwen2.5 tokenizer_config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct/raw/main/tokenizer_config.json)). **Fix:** keep each model's default, document the asymmetry, apply it identically to the baseline and map arms, and save the rendered prompts.

### I12. The ±2-point rerun tolerance uses the wrong reference

A rerun uses the same questions, so its SE is √(flip rate/n), about 0.45–0.8 points at 1–3% flips, not the binomial ~2.2. The ±2-point rule is loose for a same-config rerun, yet it can still be breached after a batch or GPU change ([Yuan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09501)). **Fix:** pin the batch size, kernel and dtype. Require an item flip rate of at most about 2% **and** a paired 95% CI of (rerun − original) inside ±1.5 points. Confirm that the verdicts and the success decision are unchanged on rerun.

## Minor: budget, parsing and reporting details

**150 max tokens is probably enough, but log truncation.** The map output is an "Answer:" line plus one copied sentence. Documents average 98.9 words, and sentences can run past 40 words. The repo audit recommended about 200 tokens because the local default is 100. **Fix:** keep 150, count `finish_reason=="length"` per position as its own failure category, and raise the cap to 200 if that rate exceeds about 1% in the pilot ([V1 guide](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/usage/v1_guide.html)).

**Map-reduce throughput is limited by decoding and fits easily.** About 12,500 prompts of roughly 600 tokens come to about 45–60 minutes for Qwen2.5-3B. Qwen3-4B takes about 1–1.5 hours, because its 144 KiB/token KV cache limits it to 35–45 concurrent sequences. A full sweep including KV takes about **1.5–2.5 hours of wall-clock** on 2×T4. These are FLOP- and bandwidth-derived estimates, anchored on 949 tok/s decoding for Qwen2.5-1.5B on a T4 ([vllm-t4-lab](https://github.com/Placidoe/vllm-t4-lab)). **Fix:** put the shared instruction first so prefix caching applies ([APC docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/automatic_prefix_caching.html)). Set `max_model_len` to about 2,048 for map runs. Checkpoint to `/kaggle/working`.

**Closed-book comparison and bootstrap mechanics.** Make "worse than closed-book" a paired per-question comparison. Also report position curves restricted to questions the model gets wrong closed-book. Use 10,000 resamples of question IDs with a fixed seed, and give McNemar exact p-values next to single-contrast CIs ([Dror et al.](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/); [Miller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)).

**Hedged answers.** Because of substring matching, an "Answer:" line like "1994 or 1995" can score as correct. Have the parser keep one short answer, or at least count lists and hedges per arm ([metrics.py](https://github.com/nelson-liu/lost-in-the-middle/blob/main/src/lost_in_the_middle/metrics.py)).

## Why the two null simulations disagree and still agree

Two independent simulations measured how the best-minus-worst gap behaves under no position effect. Their numbers differ:

| | `recent_evidence_and_stats.md` | `repo_and_code_audit.md` |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome model | Fixed/independent mixture, p=0.6 | Per-question difficulty ~ Beta(1.2, 0.8), independent Bernoulli |
| Pairwise discordance | 0.11 and 0.24 | about 0.32 |
| Mean null gap, n=500 | 2.8 and 3.6 pt | 4.15 pt |
| Mean null gap, n=2,655 | not run | 1.91 pt |
| How the CI was built | best and worst **re-chosen inside every resample**, percentile CI | best and worst **chosen once on the full data**, then that fixed pair's difference paired-bootstrapped |
| False "significant gap" rate (nominal ≤5%) | CI never covered 0 (**100%**) | CI excluded 0 in **32%** (n=500) and **35%** (n=2,655) |

The gap sizes agree once discordance is accounted for. The null gap scales roughly with √(d/n). Scaling 3.6 points at d=0.24 up to d=0.32 gives 3.6 × √(0.32/0.24) ≈ **4.2 points**, which matches 4.15. Scaling 4.15 from n=500 to n=2,655 gives 4.15 × √(500/2655) ≈ **1.8 points**, which matches 1.91. The false-positive rates differ because the two simulations test different things. Re-choosing the extremes inside each resample produces a bootstrap distribution of a max−min statistic that is bounded below by 0 and centred on its bias, so its CI cannot reach 0 at all. That is the bootstrap inconsistency for non-smooth functionals ([Andrews 2000](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-0262.00114/abstract)). Choosing once and then bootstrapping a fixed pair treats a data-selected contrast as if it were pre-specified. The CI has correct width but is centred on a winner's-curse estimate, which gives about 32% false positives instead of 5% ([Andrews, Kitagawa & McCloskey](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25456/w25456.pdf)). Both simulations simplify the dependence between positions: real discordance may be about 0.2, and correlation can vary by position. Neither changes the conclusion. Under every variant, the null gap (about 2–4 points at realistic n) is **a third to four-fifths of the 5–7 point effect expected** from recent models, and the test's false-positive rate is 6–20 times the nominal rate. The fix is the same in both cases: fixed, pre-registered contrasts, with a permutation null for any spread statistic.

## Conclusion

The plan's biggest risk is its statistics, not the free-tier hardware or the authors' artifacts. Without the fixes, three spurious effects could each produce a "success" on their own. Selection bias would show a gap shrinking by about 3 points under no effect. Slot and difficulty confounds would flatten or tilt the map-reduce curve. And the non-inferiority rule would reward noise. The mitigation itself is weaker than its description suggests. Because answer-bearing and same-topic distractors are common, the reduce step works mostly as a tie-breaker between confident candidates, not as a majority vote. How good that tie-breaker is (comparable confidence scores, or a judge) matters more than the chunking.

A negative or inconclusive result is informative here too. If the full 2,655-question baseline shows a primacy contrast of 3 points or less, the honest 2026 finding is that small Qwen models are mostly no longer lost in the middle at 3K tokens. Then the controls on distractor reduction and prompt effects, not position, explain whatever map-reduce changes. Pre-registering that outcome keeps the project publishable whichever way the pilot goes.
